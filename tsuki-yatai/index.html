    A stall that grew out of a cart

    Tsuki Yatai started as a cart with a single pot at a riverside night market: eight stools, paper lanterns and a queue until one in the morning. Chef Hana Nguyen, daughter of a Japanese ramen cook and a Vietnamese broth seller, still makes the base the family way: fourteen hours, charred onion and no shortcuts.

    We moved indoors but kept what matters: an open kitchen, a loud wok and the feeling that you ducked in for a bite somewhere between Hanoi and Osaka.

      Portfolio project

      Tsuki Yatai, made by Zakharii

      A concept website for a fictional restaurant, built from scratch with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework and no build step: open the source and every line is readable.

      What's inside

      • Scroll-driven 3D hero. A procedural noodle bowl in Three.js, pinned with GSAP ScrollTrigger, spinning and zooming with the scroll. Smooth wheel scrolling by Lenis, native touch on phones.
      • Live bowl builder. The same 3D generator rebuilds the bowl as you change the broth, noodles and toppings, with a running price.
      • Ordering flow. Add dishes, change quantities, pick a pickup time and follow a demo order. The basket is saved in the browser.
      • Menu with filters and quick view. Category tabs with keyboard support, vegetarian and spicy filters, allergen info.
      • Table booking with validation and time slots, and a live "open now" status in Swedish time.
      • Built to be fast. 3D renders only while visible, pixel ratio is capped, shadows are off on phones, images are lazy-loaded and reduced-motion is respected.
