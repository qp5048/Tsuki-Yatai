What we build

The good parts of the early web, rebuilt with today's care for speed, privacy and accessibility.

  • Guestbooks

    A page where visitors leave a kind word and a hometown. Moderated by you, readable by everyone.

  • Webrings

    Link your site with others who share a hobby. Readers hop from one to the next, no search engine needed.

  • Forums & boards

    Threads that stay in order and stay put. Your best conversations are still there in ten years.

  • Personal homepages

    Every member gets a page they can decorate. Yes, with a background tile, if they insist.

  • Chat rooms

    Small, named rooms with a friendly host. The kind where someone says hello when you come in.

  • Newsletters

    A monthly letter from the community to its members. Plain text, warmly written, easy to reply to.

How we work

Slowly enough to get it right. Most communities launch in six to ten weeks.

  1. 1

    Sit down and talk

    We meet your people, online or over coffee, and learn how they already talk to each other.

  2. 2

    Sketch on paper

    Pages are drawn by hand first. Cheap to change, easy for everyone to have an opinion on.

  3. 3

    Build it by hand

    Plain HTML, careful CSS and only the JavaScript it needs. Fast on old phones and slow connections.

  4. 4

    Tend it together

    We train your moderators and stay on for a season, fixing things as the community grows.

Featured community

Mossboard: 1,200 gardeners, one forum

A Swedish gardening club had outgrown its Facebook group. Posts vanished in the feed and half the members had quit the app. We built them a forum that works like a well-kept allotment: everything in its place, nothing selling anything.

  • Threads sorted by last reply, never by "engagement"
  • Member homepages with photos of their plots
  • A seed-swap guestbook and a gardening webring
  • Loads in under a second on a ten-year-old phone

Why small networks feel better

When a place has a few hundred people instead of a few hundred million, it behaves differently. People are kinder when they will see each other again next week.

  • Chronological, always. Newest at the top. You decide what's important.
  • No tracking. No ad pixels, no data brokers, no "suggested for you".
  • The community owns it. Your domain, your data, exportable any day.
  • Light as a leaf. Works on old computers, slow trains and cheap phones.

Questions people ask

Didn't find yours? Leave it in the guestbook below.

Is this really like the 90s web?

In spirit, yes: small, personal and chronological. In practice it's modern: it works on phones, it's accessible to screen readers and it's secure.

Can our members log in with Google or Facebook?

We prefer a simple email login, so no big platform learns who belongs to your community. If you really need it, we can add it.

Who moderates the community?

Your own people. We set up the tools and train your moderators, then stay around for a season in case anything gets thorny.

What happens if you disappear?

You own the code, the domain and the data from day one. Any web developer can pick it up; it's plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript.
